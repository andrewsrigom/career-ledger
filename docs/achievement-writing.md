# Achievement writing framework

Career Ledger uses an evidence-safe variation of the XYZ pattern:

> Accomplished **X**, demonstrated by **Y**, by doing **Z**.

The structure is useful, but evidence remains more important than completing every placeholder.

## X — accomplishment or result

Lead with the durable change: what became possible, what was delivered, or what problem was removed.

Good examples name a concrete system or capability. Avoid vague statements such as “worked on,” “helped with,” or “improved” without explaining what changed.

## Y — evidence or measurement

Add a number, observed behavior, public release, adoption signal, or owner-confirmed outcome only when the evidence supports it.

If no reliable measurement exists, omit the metric. Never invent percentages, time savings, scale, reliability, adoption, or business impact to make the sentence fit the framework.

## Z — contribution or method

Explain the technical decisions and implementation that produced the result. Prefer a small number of coherent contributions over a feature inventory.

## Page mapping

The project-first homepage maps the framework to visible sections:

- **Project** establishes the system and its purpose.
- **Record** states the activity, notable contribution, milestone, or achievement at the evidence-supported significance level.
- **Results** shows only outcomes with known evidence and appears first.
- **Key work** records the most important technical contributions and method.

The homepage intentionally limits the implementation list to three key contributions. Detail pages retain the complete record, but the homepage must contain enough information to understand the project without opening them. Do not label every related record as an achievement.
